Where Parts Come From
Parts only enter play as Part pickups (Assets/Prefabs/Entities/Collectable/Part.prefab, TransferrableParts: 1 each) scattered into the world on destruction, then collected by flying into them or via the Magnet upgrade (Collectible.OnCollected() → Ship.AddParts()). There is no other way to gain Parts during a run.

Ship.Destruct() is shared by every Ship component, so it also fires for the player's own death (PartsDrop: 0 on the Player prefab — no drop, since the run is over anyway). Per-prefab drop counts (PartsDrop/PartsVariance on both Asteroid and Ship — PartsDrop is the average drop count, PartsVariance the ± swing around it) are Inspector-tunable on each enemy/asteroid prefab. Asteroid's formula clamps the lower bound at 0 and rolls inclusive of both ends (int overload of Random.Range); Ship's PartsDrop is a float, so it uses the inclusive-both-ends float overload directly.
The only non-pickup source: Sector Achievements, awarded directly to Parts (no Part pickup involved) on the Sector Achievements screen between sectors, once per sector per achievement.

Resources sink
Spends Parts to top off a resource's current value toward its cap. Does not increase the cap itself. Repeatable any number of times per visit, as long as the resource isn't already full.
RowCostEffectMethod
Health 5 parts +20% of Max Health, instantly Ship.ConvertPartsToHealth()
Fuel 5 parts +20% of Max Fuel, instantly Ship.ConvertPartsToFuel()
Ammo 5 parts +20% of Max Ammo, instantly Ship.ConvertPartsToAmmo()
Cost (PartsConversionCost, 5) and percent (PartsConversionPercent, 20%) are shared, flat values — they don't scale with repeated use or upgrade level. The percentage automatically scales with each stat's current cap, so buying a Max Fuel/Ammo/Health upgrade also makes these refills more effective per purchase.
Ship Upgrades / Research sink
Permanent stat increases. Levels and Parts spent persist across sector reloads and saves (via GameControl / PlayerStatistics) — they are not lost when starting a new sector, only reset on New Game.
Fire Rate, Max Health, Firepower, Max Fuel, Max Ammo unlimited levels
These five share one cost formula and have no level cap — buy as many levels as you can afford, forever. The first two levels are flat, easily-attainable steps (StatUpgradeFirstLevelCost, StatUpgradeSecondLevelCost) so the climb into the geometric progression isn't a steep 5→30 jump; the established progression resumes unchanged from level 2→3 onward:
cost(0→1) = StatUpgradeFirstLevelCost = 5
cost(1→2) = StatUpgradeSecondLevelCost = 15
cost(level→level+1), level ≥ 2 = round( StatUpgradeBaseCost × StatUpgradeCostGrowth level )
= round( 25 × 1.2 level )

Base values for Max Health/Firepower/Max Fuel/Max Ammo on the Player prefab are set one delta below what they used to be — buying the cheap level-1 upgrade brings a new player right back to the old starting point (e.g. Max Health 90 + 10 = 100), so the cheap first purchase reads as a deliberate early goal rather than a balance change. Fire Rate has no such history — PrimaryCoolDown 0.25s is simply the prefab's shipped default, since this upgrade replaced Speed rather than inheriting a prior baseline of its own. Per-level deltas (FireRateCoolDownDelta, HealthUpgradeDelta, FirePowerUpgradeDelta, FuelUpgradeDelta, AmmoUpgradeDelta) and the shared StatUpgradeBaseCost / StatUpgradeCostGrowth / StatUpgradeFirstLevelCost / StatUpgradeSecondLevelCost are all Inspector-tunable on the Player prefab's Ship component.
Magnet (Pickup Attraction) 30 levels
Pulls nearby Collectibles (Parts and Relics) toward the ship within a radius, flying them directly at the player with a ramping speed (Collectible.Reel()) rather than a physics push. The first three levels ramp up cheaply (5 / 10 / 15) before settling into the flat 20-parts-per-level cost; radius grows +1 per level.

Homing 30 levels
No longer bends the primary Missile's path itself — each level instead raises the odds that firing a Missile also launches a separate, always-homing Homing Rocket alongside it (no extra ammo cost). Same cost curve as Magnet (5 / 10 / 15, then flat 20) and the same 30-level cap; only the proc chance improves per level — the rocket's turn rate is a flat constant, not a per-level scale.

The Homing Rocket's turn rate (HomingRocketTurnRate, 400°/s) is a flat constant at every level — it doesn't scale with level. The rocket flies straight for a flat HomingDelay (default 0.75s) before it's even eligible to home, and only actually starts homing once a target is within HomingRange (default 8 units) — both gates must pass. Method: Ship.UpgradeHoming().
Sector Exploration sink
Specific to the current sector only — does not carry over when the sector advances or reloads. All four rows below share the same one-time-per-sector, plain-instance-field-on-Ship shape rather than persisting via GameControl/PlayerStatistics.
RowCostEffectMethod
Portal Scan 30 parts Immediately reveals the sector's portal location on the radar, without needing to find it by exploring Ship.ScanForPortal()
Enemy Locator 30 parts Shows off-screen direction indicators for hostile blips beyond radar range. In-range enemies already show on the radar regardless — this only extends visibility to ones currently off-screen. Ship.PurchaseEnemyLocator()
Asteroid Locator 30 parts Same as Enemy Locator, but for asteroid blips. Ship.PurchaseAsteroidLocator()
Parts Locator 30 parts Unlike the other two, Part pickups don't appear on the radar at all until this is purchased — not even in-range ones. Once purchased, both in-range and off-screen Parts blips show. Ship.PurchasePartsLocator()
Portal Scan disables once the portal has already been discovered (Ship.IsPortalDiscovered()), whether by scanning or by flying close enough to it naturally. The other three are one-time per-sector purchases gated by their own _enemyLocatorPurchased / _asteroidLocatorPurchased / _partsLocatorPurchased flag (LocatorScanCost, 30, shared by all three) — VectorHudController.RefreshBlips() reads each flag to decide whether to draw that blip kind's off-screen direction arrows (or, for Parts, whether to draw the blip at all).
Planned / Backlog
Tracked in BACKLOG.md. Not yet implemented — shown here so the full economy, shipped and planned, stays visible in one place.
Planned sources

Equipment Upgrade Sell-Back planned

Downgrade any purchased upgrade for a partial refund (60–75% of cost), spendable immediately elsewhere. A reverse flow on top of every sink above, not a new pickup type.

Harvest Sector Type planned

Part of Sector Type Variants — a sector variant focused on parts/resource collection rather than clearing hostiles, giving Parts a dedicated gameplay loop instead of only coming as a kill byproduct.

Planned sinks

Parts Capacity planned

Parts currently have no maximum — Ship.AddParts() never clamps. Planned: a purchasable MaxParts cap (same escalating-cost pattern as the other capacity upgrades). Once at the cap, Part pickups should stop being collected and stop being pulled in by Magnet, so the player can visibly see they're full and has to spend down before picking up more.

Shield System planned

A blockable-damage shield with tiers (10% / 25% / 50% block chance) purchasable via parts investment, with its own stat row in Ship Management.
