No missile weapon — Enemy.TryFire() is a no-op override. Every Beam Interval, if the player is within Firing Range, fires a beam that snapshots this satellite's current facing (transform.up) at the moment of firing and stays locked to that direction for the full Beam Duration; the satellite also freezes in place (no orbit/rotation) while the beam is active. Homing Rocket
Missile.cs · DestroyableObject
(
U+0028 · Infinity Icons HU
Combat
Damage = FirePower
TTL 7s
Damage multiplier 2× (Ship.HomingRocketDamageMultiplier)
Same Missile.cs script and stats as the regular Missile (it is one, with a baked-in homing config) — only the glyph visual and the always-on steering-toward-nearest-target behavior differ. Targets the nearest Enemy or Asteroid, whichever is closer. Damage still rolls off Ship's FirePower/FirePowerVariance like every shot, then gets multiplied by HomingRocketDamageMultiplier so the rocket hits harder than the Missile it accompanies.
Homing Upgrade (player only)
Proc chance 2% → 30% (Lv 1–30)
Turn rate 400°/s (constant, all levels)
Homing delay / range 0.75s / 8 units
Replaces the old behavior where the Homing upgrade bent the player's regular Missile. Each Missile shot now independently rolls the Lv-based proc chance; on a hit, a Homing Rocket fires alongside the Missile at no extra ammo cost. Only the proc chance scales with level (30 levels, mirroring Magnet's cost curve and cap) -- the rocket's turn rate is a flat constant regardless of level, so the rocket itself is always equally capable, only rarer at low levels. The rocket flies straight at launch (no force/turn applied) for a flat HomingDelay, and even past that delay keeps flying straight until a target comes within HomingRange -- so it reads as ballistic at range and only locks on once a target is close. The rocket turns via Rigidbody2D.MoveRotation, and once locked on, redirects its existing velocity vector onto the new facing every FixedUpdate rather than accelerating toward a fixed speed -- it keeps exactly whatever speed it already had at launch (ship momentum included) for its whole flight, only ever changing heading, so locking onto a target never reads as a sudden speed change. The homing FixedUpdate loop (and its velocity) stops the instant the rocket takes damage/explodes, instead of continuing to steer during the 0.2s explosion-to-destroy window. Ship.cs: HomingProcChances / HomingRocketTurnRate / HomingDelay / HomingRange.
